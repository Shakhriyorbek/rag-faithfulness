\movedsub{The same probe with entities instead of numbers}
\label{sec:entity}

A numeric substitution is one error type, and the pattern above could be specific to it. The same construction runs on named entities: take a grounded entity in the answer, replace it with a donor entity that the context does not contain, hold everything else fixed. Cases are built once and every evaluator sees the same ones. Table~\ref{tab:entity} reports them.

The verbosity effect survives the change of error type. Under the entailment aggregate on Natural Questions a falsified entity moves the score by 0.082 for the most verbose generator and 0.403 for the tersest, against 0.054 and 0.451 for numbers. Detection at the fixed gate is 9.1\% against 48.8\%, against 5.1\% and 56.6\%. The gap narrows slightly and does not close.

Ordering by generator holds in three of the six dataset-evaluator combinations, against five of six when only three generators were compared. Both failures the fourth generator introduces are transpositions of Qwen and Mistral-7B under NLI-max, and on the numeric probe those two sit within 0.03 of each other (35.1\% against 34.3\% detection on Natural Questions, 54.8\% against 52.5\% on HotpotQA), so little is needed to reorder them. On the entity probe the Natural Questions swap is not small: Mistral-7B detects 26.9\% of falsified entities and Qwen 10.8\%. The remaining failure, present with three generators too, is claim-min on Natural Questions, where Claude is scored \emph{more} sensitively than Qwen (0.163 against 0.112, detection 25.9\% against 17.9\%). The ordering of the extremes survives throughout. Assertion count predicts the ordering well but it does not fix it.

Entity and numeric substitution are not interchangeable, and neither is uniformly the harder case. Both agree closely for GPT-4o-mini and Mistral-7B, whose mean drops under the two error types differ by at most 0.048 and 0.052 across all six cells. They diverge for Claude and Qwen, and in opposite directions. On HotpotQA the entailment aggregate responds roughly twice as strongly to a falsified entity as to a falsified number for the verbose generator (0.178 against 0.085). On Natural Questions the mid-length generator shows the reverse, by a wider factor (0.088 against 0.261). A claim that an evaluator does or does not catch fabrications is therefore a claim about a particular kind of fabrication. The two error types we tested do not license extrapolation to each other. They certainly do not license it for unsupported causal claims or contradicted negations, which we did not test.
