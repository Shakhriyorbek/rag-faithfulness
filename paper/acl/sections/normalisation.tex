\movedsub{A normalisation defect that produced a result}
\label{sec:normalisation}

During development, before the normalisation was fixed, this analysis found two of the four cells then in the design breaking rather than one. The difference was a change of text normalisation, not of test, data or population. The entailment scorer read the answer as the generator wrote it, while the claim-level scorer stripped markdown first, so the two aggregates were not reading the same hypothesis. Markdown is unevenly distributed, appearing in 76.0\% of Claude's attempted answers against 1.3\%, 0.3\% and 0.2\% for Qwen, GPT-4o-mini and Mistral-7B, so one generator's formatting habits were entering a comparison between embedding systems. Stripping it for all three evaluators removed the significance of NQ/Claude under AlignScore. That comparison is $p = 0.0081$ uncorrected and 0.154 after correction over the twenty-four in Table~\ref{tab:7}. None of the four comparisons that now survive is affected, since all are on generators that put markdown in at most 1.3\% of their attempted answers.

How it was caught is the transferable part. The defect is invisible in every summary statistic and survives every significance test. What exposed it was an identity the two aggregates must satisfy exactly on single-assertion answers. It failed on all 131 formatted answers then in the grid, by as much as 0.729 (Appendix~\ref{app:markdown}). Metrics that admit such an identity should be checked against it.
