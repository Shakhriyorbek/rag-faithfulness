\movedsub{Membership is set by a grader, and graders disagree}
\label{sec:graders}

Excluding refusals means deciding which answers are refusals, and a rule decides it. We compare two: the rule every evaluator score in this paper rests on, which matches a list of declining openings wider than the prompt's own refusal sentence after stripping a leading attribution clause (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), and the refusal classification of a judge model shown the question, the reference answers and the answer. They agree on most rows. The judge finds up to 3.4 more refusals per hundred answers in a system, and on one system 0.5 fewer, and it reorders the four systems by refusal rate in none of the eight dataset-generator cells. The comparison below covers all twenty-four dataset-generator-evaluator comparisons, and the graded-correctness results of \movedref~\ref{sec:refusals} cover all thirty-two dataset-generator-system cells.

A level shift that preserves the ordering is usually unremarkable. This one is not, because the rows it moves sit at the boundary between refusing and attempting. Swapping detectors changes the status of 68 rows on NQ/Claude and 173 on HotpotQA/Claude, and of between 5 and 317 per dataset-generator cell across the grid. In two of the twenty-four comparisons that carries the uncorrected p across 0.05: NQ/Claude under the entailment aggregate from 0.155 to 0.032, and HotpotQA/Claude under AlignScore from 0.063 to 0.022. Neither survives correction. The four comparisons that do survive are the same under both, and under the rule stripped of its two extensions (Appendix~\ref{app:refusalrule}), so we report the corrected count and treat any single comparison near the uncorrected threshold as undetermined.
