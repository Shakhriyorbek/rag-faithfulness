\movedsub{Choosing among the three}
\label{sec:choosing}

The recommendations above say how to report a faithfulness result. They do not say which evaluator to run, and nothing in this paper supports a single answer to that. The three differ on properties that matter different amounts in different applications. Table~\ref{tab:choose} collects what we measured into the form that decision needs. Every entry comes from a table earlier in the paper; none of it is new evidence.

Read it against the deployment, not in the abstract. If answers are short and the question is whether a fabricated value gets caught, the deterministic check dominates and the choice of entailment scorer barely matters. If answers are long, no evaluator here catches most fabrications. The gap between the best and worst of them is still the difference between a weak gate and no gate: 24.1\% against 5.1\% on the verbose generator's NQ cell. If the evaluation population cannot be guaranteed free of refusals, AlignScore is the only one of the three that scores refusals below attempted answers for every generator we ran. Under the other two, refusals score above real answers for two generators and far below for the third, so pooling them moves the average in a generator-dependent direction. If systems are being compared and the expected difference is small, the relevant property is resolving power, where AlignScore needs 0.012 to 0.037 against 0.021 to 0.060 for the entailment aggregate at the same sample size.

Those criteria do not agree, and we are not proposing a weighting for them. The point of stating them together is that a choice is being made whenever a faithfulness number is reported, and it is usually made by default.
