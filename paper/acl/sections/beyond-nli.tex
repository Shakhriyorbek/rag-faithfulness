\movedsub{Does this reach past entailment scorers?}
\label{sec:beyond-nli}

Two of the three evaluators we test are built on the same entailment model, which invites the reading that we have diagnosed NLI rather than faithfulness measurement. Other paradigms exist. ARES \citep{saad-falcon-etal-2024} fine-tunes lightweight judges on synthetic data and calibrates them against a few hundred human annotations with prediction-powered inference. RAGVUE \citep{murugaraj-etal-2026} decomposes a RAG system into retrieval quality, answer relevance, claim-level faithfulness and judge calibration, and attaches an explanation to each. RAG-Zeval \citep{li-etal-2025-rag} trains a compact evaluator with reinforcement learning to judge faithfulness and correctness in one reasoning pass, rewarding it on rankings of responses rather than on absolute scores. We ran none of the three, so what follows is an argument about what our measurements imply for them, not a test of them.

Three of our results do not depend on entailment. AlignScore is not an NLI model. It is an alignment function trained across factual-consistency tasks, and in the verbosity control it loses more than either entailment aggregate: detection falls from 74.5\% to 18.5\% on HotpotQA, and its mean response to a falsified value drops from 0.494 to 0.127 (Section~\ref{sec:verbosity}). The structural bias therefore attaches to scalar aggregation over a multi-assertion answer, not to the entailment model underneath.

We tested how far that reaches with a generative fact-checker, Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B, scoring the whole answer as one claim (Appendix~\ref{app:minicheck}). It is a much stronger instrument on this task. It catches 80.6\% to 97.5\% of falsified values across the four generators, and the distance between the least and most detected generator falls from over 50 points under NLI-max to about 15. The direction survives. Claude is still the least detected generator on both datasets, and the within-model verbosity arm still costs it 31 and 40 points of detection, from 95.9\% to 65.2\% and from 91.0\% to 51.0\%. So the level of the bias depends on the scorer, and a trained checker reduces it sharply; the dependence on how much the answer asserts is present in every scorer we ran.

Claim-level decomposition is the obvious response and is what RAGVUE prescribes. We measure it. Claim-min is the most length-robust of the three. It gives up 36 detection points between the extreme generators, against 54 and 46 for the others, and it holds 43.6\% detection where the others fall below 19\%. It is also the aggregate whose absolute scores collapse at that verbosity, to means of 0.234 and 0.076, so decomposition buys sensitivity and costs thresholdability. That trade follows from taking a minimum rather than from the model taking it, so we would expect it in any claim-decomposed metric that aggregates by minimum. We tested two. MiniCheck's own per-sentence minimum keeps detection at 94\% to 99\% in every cell, verbosity arm included, while the share of the verbose arm's untouched answers passing the 0.5 gate falls to 32\% and 16\%.

Calibration is a different axis from the one we measure, which is why ARES is complementary rather than an answer. Prediction-powered inference corrects a judge's bias against a human-labelled sample: it makes the reported number closer to what annotators would say on that distribution. Our two failure modes survive that correction. A judge can be unbiased on average and still fail to separate two systems at the sample sizes used here, four hundred to eight hundred paired queries per cell. That is resolving power (Appendix~\ref{app:power}). It can equally be unbiased on average and still move by 0.054 when a grounded value is replaced with a false one. That is sensitivity to a known error. Neither is a calibration error, and neither would be visible in agreement with annotators on unperturbed answers.

What would test this properly is running these frameworks through the same probe. The MiniCheck result makes a prediction for them: a trained judge will sit at a higher level than an entailment aggregate, and its detection will still fall when the same generator writes more. We expect the calibration machinery to be orthogonal to that, and we have not shown it.
